The Soap Bubble Theorem: Why Mass Concentrates on the Equator & Outer Shell

In standard 3D Euclidean space, a solid ball contains significant interior volume. But as dimensionality \(d\) approaches 1,536, the fraction of volume in the core sphere of radius \((1 - \epsilon)\) collapses exponentially:

\(\lim_{d \to \infty} \frac{\text{Vol}(B^d(1 - \epsilon))}{\text{Vol}(B^d(1))} = (1 - \epsilon)^d \approx 0\) ε = 0.01, d=1536

For \(d = 1536\) and \(\epsilon = 0.01\), \((0.99)^{1536} \approx 2.14 \times 10^{-7}\). Almost 99.9999% of the volume of the hyper-sphere resides in an unimaginably thin outer shell within 1% of the surface!

Consequently, all transformer token embeddings are effectively orthogonal to one another by default unless deliberate semantic co-occurrence binds them together into dense, low-dimensional manifold ribbons.
